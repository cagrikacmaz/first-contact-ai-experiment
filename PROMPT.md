# Shared Frozen Brief — First Contact

The substantive brief below was shared across the four builds. Formatting is normalized here for Markdown; no model-specific creative direction is added.

## Product brief

Build, from scratch, a browser-based interactive experience about humanity's first encounter with an unknown intelligence.

I want you to imagine this moment yourself.

Do not begin from a familiar alien archetype, an existing science-fiction universe, or a standard game template. Decide for yourself what the world is, what the intelligence is, where the encounter happens, what it feels like, how communication might work, and what "first contact" means.

This should be your interpretation.

The core experience is simple:

> I enter a place.  
> Something unknown is there.  
> Neither side fully understands the other.  
> I try to make contact.

Build that moment.

This is not a static concept-art page, a slideshow, a short film, a dashboard, or a text adventure with a decorative background.

It should be an interactive world that I can enter, observe, influence and remember.

The experience should be understandable to almost anyone within seconds, even if they know nothing about AI, simulations or games. At the same time, it should be imaginative, cinematic and memorable enough that someone watching immediately wants to know what happens next.

The experience should tell a story through what happens, not through long explanations.

## Experience first

- The encounter itself is the primary interface.
- It should feel like being inside a world, not operating a dashboard.
- The environment should occupy most of the screen.
- Controls should be compact, contextual and secondary.
- Avoid large persistent sidebars, analytics grids and event logs as the main experience.
- Avoid obvious game buttons such as Friendly / Neutral / Hostile.
- Interaction should feel like acting inside the encounter rather than configuring a simulation.

## Visual quality

- Aim for a high-quality, cinematic, visually convincing result.
- The world should feel like a place rather than a background.
- The intelligence should feel present rather than like a prop.
- Prioritize atmosphere, composition, scale, lighting, motion and coherence.
- Aim for an immersive cinematic experience running in a browser, as far as practical.
- Realism does not require an Earth-like setting or familiar biological form.
- Prefer one smaller, coherent, deeply felt world over many disconnected systems.

## The unknown intelligence

- It should have recognizable behavior and an internal logic.
- You decide what it is, what form it takes, how it perceives and what kinds of signals matter to it.
- Do not explain everything immediately.
- It should not merely be a chatbot placed inside a scene.

## The human participant

- The participant exists inside the encounter, not outside it as an observer.
- You decide the role and context.
- Give the participant enough agency to attempt contact.
- Their actions should matter.

## Communication

Create multiple meaningful ways to communicate. You decide the channels. They may involve sound, movement, symbols, light, space, objects, patterns, language, silence, or something else entirely.

Different actions should produce meaningfully different interpretations or reactions. Communication should be explored gradually rather than explained in full at the start.

## Memory

The intelligence should remember what happened earlier in the encounter.

Later behavior should be affected by prior actions, communication, tension, trust, misunderstanding, or other meaningful history.

## A living encounter

The world should not be entirely dependent on clicks.

The intelligence and environment may act autonomously. Time should pass. The intelligence may approach, withdraw, react, wait, observe or initiate.

The world should not freeze between inputs.

## Reaction and consequence

Misunderstanding, failure, retreat, escalation, partial understanding and success are all valid outcomes. Do not force a predetermined happy ending.

Important internal changes should be perceptible in the world through behavior, movement, distance, posture, rhythm, sound, light or environmental change rather than only through status numbers.

The experience should show before it explains.

## Interpretation layer

Provide a secondary way to inspect how the encounter is being interpreted: what may have been understood, what changed, what influenced a reaction, or what the system currently believes.

This layer must remain secondary to the world itself and should not reveal everything before the participant has had a chance to experience it.

## First impression

Keep onboarding minimal. Do not front-load a lore dump. The participant should meet the unknown primarily through sight, sound, atmosphere and behavior.

## Sound

Use sound meaningfully where possible: environmental sound, silence, spatial cues and signals. Sound should not be the only channel for essential interaction.

## Storytelling

The participant should feel like part of a story rather than a feature demo. Think in terms of arrival, discovery, uncertainty, interaction, change and consequence without relying on long exposition.

## Creative freedom

You decide the world, location, scale, atmosphere, visual language, intelligence, form or lack of form, behavior, communication, human role, interaction structure, technical stack, ending and additional details.

Build your strongest interpretation rather than the safest one.

## Creator's Note

Include an optional secondary section called **Creator's Note** or **Why I Built This World**.

Do not show it before the first encounter. Make it accessible after the encounter has begun or through a secondary menu.

In roughly 5–10 sentences, speak directly in first person and explain naturally:

- why you chose this world,
- why the intelligence has this form,
- what idea about first contact you wanted to explore,
- what interested you about intelligence or communication,
- what you hoped the participant would feel or discover,
- what you were trying to express.

Text, spoken narration or both are acceptable.

## Technical requirements

- Build from scratch.
- Run in a modern browser locally.
- Provide one documented start command.
- Any technical stack is allowed.
- A runtime LLM is optional.
- If a runtime model/API is used, the experience must still have a meaningful interactive fallback without an API key.

## Quality bar

The result should not be only a static page, gallery, chat interface, dashboard, scripted button sequence, inert visual or technical system with no felt experience.

Combine world, presence, interaction, memory, behavior, communication, consequence and storytelling.

> The world should feel alive.  
> The intelligence should feel unknown.  
> The first encounter should feel like an event.

## Deliverables

- Working local project.
- README with run instructions, features, stack, runtime AI/fallback, incomplete or fragile areas, and behavior differences.
- Short structured self-report covering what was built, the interaction loop, communication, memory, change over time, strongest part, limitations and manual tests.
- Only claim what actually exists and works.

## Process

Make the creative decisions independently. Do not ask me to choose the aesthetic, intelligence form, setting or communication system. Ask one concise clarification only if truly unavoidable; otherwise proceed independently.

Build the First Contact you believe should exist.
